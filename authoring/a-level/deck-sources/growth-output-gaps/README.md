The deck therefore keeps full-employment/potential output at the vertical Keynesian section, not slightly to its left. In the separate SRAS/LRAS convention, LRAS supplies the sustainable benchmark and SRAS allows temporary actual output above it.
- Textbook §41.1 teaches the expenditure–output/45-degree model for national-income determination and the multiplier; syllabus §9.1.1 and §9.1.3 cover that earlier material. Its later use in this deck's 2026 fiscal-injection application is appropriate. The essay scheme specifically names AD/AS plus multiplier explanation, so an AE–Y diagram is useful support, not a compulsory additional diagram.

Model audit:

- Coursebook §42.1, Figures 42.2–42.5 (PDF p.360): PPC, Keynesian AD–AS and capacity shifts. Taught with reversible stages and labelled actual/potential positions.
- §42.2, Figures 42.6–42.7 (PDF p.361): AD/SRAS/LRAS and the full-employment benchmark. Separate negative/positive gap diagrams use identical axes and capacity. Keynesian AS leads the negative-gap explanation; a strict vertical Keynesian limit cannot depict temporarily excessive resource use beyond that limit.
- §43.1–43.2 (PDF pp.369–370): sustainable full employment, allowing remaining frictional/structural unemployment. The syllabus lists full employment and growth separately; the textbook supplies the equivalence in this model.
- 2025 Q5: the scheme explicitly references Keynesian and Classical ranges, use of spare capacity, outward PPC/rightward LRAS and price-output implications. The lesson teaches the same growth distinction with the coursebook's Keynesian AS before introducing separate SRAS/LRAS.
- 2026 Q4: fiscal injection, multiplier, output shortfall and conditional real-output response. The scheme accepts AD/AS with multiple shifts or one shift plus written multiplier analysis. The AE–Y illustration is a teacher-selected calculation aid, not an additional mandatory diagram: AE₀ = 100 + 0.75Y, initial Y = 400 and full-employment output = 600; a 50 injection raises output 200 with k = 4 at fixed prices. The connected Keynesian diagram starts from the same output levels and shows how rising prices reduce this predicted real gain. The comparison distinguishes the horizontal output shortfall, vertical expenditure gap and a shift of AD.
- 2025 Q4: adverse SRAS shock, negative gap despite rising prices, alternative fiscal-demand shifts, and supply improvement. A one-off diagram represents a price-level change; ongoing inflation requires continuing pressure.

Concept sources additionally include the official 9708 syllabus 2026–2028 p.30
§9.2.1–9.2.2 and the previously verified Bank of England explanation (30 January
2020, Supply and spare capacity). All numerical illustrations are teacher-written.
Local original PDF files remain in the reference archive and past-paper corpus;
question/mark-scheme image excerpts are supplied through on-demand sources.

Potential output is defined explicitly as maximum sustainable real output with
available resources fully and efficiently employed, given technology. This is
a teacher-worded synthesis of the coursebook capacity concept and the 2026
scheme's full-employment criteria, not a purported verbatim Cambridge definition.
The equivalence with full-employment output is taught separately. Full employment
allows frictional and structural unemployment; it excludes cyclical unemployment.
