Communication job: By the end, Cambridge A Level Economics students should explain what changes each
component of aggregate demand, distinguish autonomous from induced expenditure, and connect those
changes to the already-taught multiplier and the accelerator.

Numerical teaching models
- Consumption: C = 100 + 0.8Y_d; therefore S = -100 + 0.2Y_d and break-even Y_d = 500.
- Accelerator: output in £bn is 100, 110, 125, 135, 140, 140 with v = 2; induced I is
  unknown, 20, 30, 20, 10, 0 (£bn). Year 1 cannot be inferred without prior output.
  Replacement investment is excluded from this model, but taught in the factory example.
- Autonomous investment diagram reuses the previous lesson's AE = 100 + 0.75Y model so students
  can distinguish the £50 vertical shift from the later multiplied change in income.

Accelerator revision, 8 September 2026
- Coursebook section 41.2 (PDF p.354) explicitly teaches the accelerator coefficient.
  Syllabus 9.1.2 names the accelerator but does not separately prescribe coefficient
  calculations. Retain one brief coefficient example after the factory mechanism.
- Factory table adapts the first five years of coursebook Table 41.1. Students trace
  demand -> required stock -> extra machines -> replacements -> total purchases.
- Prioritise the stock/flow distinction, volatility, changes in annual additions,
  spare capacity and expectations, and the direction of multiplier/accelerator causation.
- Equal absolute additions imply constant induced investment in the fixed-ratio model;
  do not imply that a constant positive percentage growth rate gives equal additions.
- For the existing 1.5-lesson allocation, use definition, falling-investment and 2026
  table questions in class; the 2025 smaller-rise question is independent consolidation.
  The coefficient and revealed table feedback can be brief or used for support.
- The 2026 Paper 4 fiscal-policy question found in the corpus depends on deflationary
  gaps (9.1.3), so it is not inserted into this components-of-AD lesson.
