Communication job: By the end, Cambridge A Level Economics students should explain the multiplier process,
calculate it in four economy models, calculate average and marginal propensities, determine equilibrium
national income with the income approach, and evaluate the real-output effect of a change in AD. Lesson 1
ends after multiplier size and calculation. Lesson 2 starts with national income determination.

Sequence evidence
- AS & A Economics Syllabus 2026-2028.pdf, subject-content p.29: section 9.1.1 specifies the
  multiplier definition; formulae and calculation in closed and open economies with and without
  government; average and marginal propensities; national income determination using AD and the
  income approach; and calculation of the effect of changing AD on national income.

Numerical teaching models
- Spending rounds: initial injection £100m, MPC 0.75, total extra income £400m.
- Marginal-flow table: change in income £200m; MPC 0.70, MPS 0.10, MRT 0.15, MPM 0.05;
  MPW 0.30 and k 3.33 recurring.
- Income approach: C = 0.75Y, original I £100m and equilibrium Y £400m. A £50m rise in I
  raises equilibrium Y to £600m. A £50m fall in I lowers equilibrium Y to £200m.
- Lesson 2 derives AE in stages: planned expenditure and stock signals; the C = 0.75Y function;
  the autonomous I = £100m function; the vertical sum AE = C + I; the 45° equality line; and
  equilibrium adjustment below and above Y = £400m.
- Successive AD shifts represent cumulative positions after spending rounds. The illustrated increments
  are £50m, £37.5m and £28.125m, so each induced shift is 0.75 of the previous shift.
- Prices remain fixed in the income-expenditure model. The AD/AS slides then show why rising prices
  and limited spare capacity can reduce the real-output response below the fixed-price result.
