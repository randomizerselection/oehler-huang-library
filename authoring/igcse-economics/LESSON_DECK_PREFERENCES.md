# IGCSE Economics lesson-deck preferences

Updated 9 September 2026 from the user's explicit feedback on Banking lesson 3. Apply these to future IGCSE decks and relevant revisions; do not silently redesign every older lesson. Preserve the Economics course's own renderer, navy/cyan/gold style, selective Chinese support and answer reveals.

## Syllabus and Paper 2 first

- Read the relevant syllabus requirements and corresponding real Paper 2 questions and mark schemes before choosing knowledge points, terminology, lists or lesson structure.
- Use accepted mark-scheme wording where available. Avoid invented canonical lists such as “three core bank services” when the schemes accept a broader set. Distinguish a function from examples of that function.
- Name knowledge slides after the actual concept or relationship: “Functions of a commercial bank”, “Functions of a central bank”, “Types of bank lending”, “Competition and choice of bank”. Avoid vague labels such as “Lending to households” for a product comparison, “Attracting customers” as a section name, or editorial titles such as “From a service to a benefit”. Preserve exact wording inside real exam questions.
- Teach operations and importance explicitly, then give real past-paper questions, independent attempts and complete teacher-written model answers grounded in the actual scheme. Reveal model paragraphs separately and show how creditworthy explanations develop.
- Do not invent a universal mark allocation or paragraph formula. Preserve full original question wording and paper/question references.
- Reserve cause-and-effect flows for relevant economic explanation/analysis in Paper 2. A payment service is examinable; tracing an operational account transfer is not a separate analysis target for Banking.
- Loan, overdraft and mortgage are explicit examples in 2025ON-23 Q3(c). Teach their uses within lending; do not treat them as three different banking functions or overteach a product taxonomy.

## Lesson organisation

- Split substantial topics into separate coherent lessons. Banking is two decks: Commercial banks, then Central banks. Each deck has its own objectives, recall, exit and study views.
- Within a lesson, use sections for definition, functions and the remaining specific knowledge points (for example competition and choice of bank, or importance). Do not put both types of bank in a single long teaching deck.

## Questions and slide types

- Prioritise suitable real Cambridge Paper 1 questions for MCQs over made-up questions. Check original stems, options and official answer keys, preserve option order, and record the paper/session/question reference. Use teacher-written questions only when there is a clear teaching need that the available past papers do not meet; label adaptations honestly. Select questions that fit the content already taught.
- Omit low-value discussion and generic follow-up activities that repeat the learning already achieved by teaching and model answers. In Commercial banks, do not restore the Mei borrow-now/save-first discussion or “Improve the explanation” slide. A discussion does not earn its place merely by offering a trade-off.
- Remove the “Pair task” layout entirely, including its use for recall. Use independent written checks with revealed models, image-background discussion or another appropriate established layout.
- Every discussion prompt should include a complete suggested answer using the existing Show possible answer interaction; keep the answer hidden until requested.
- Four-mark Explain two plans should show two identification/explanation pairs matching the scheme, e.g. Identify role [1] alongside Explain role for firms [1], repeated as a second row. Avoid a three-step “name, explain, repeat” plan. Keep plans visible when the question appears.
- Tables should reveal one body row per click; retain visible column headers and complete tables in handout/print view.
- Discontinue “Complete the missing sentence” / missingSentence peer tasks. Use short written explanations, plausible MCQs, error correction and classify-and-justify work with hidden model feedback.
- Avoid easy yes/no and thumbs-up questions. Require application, a reason, a meaningful comparison, or correction of a plausible misconception.
- Use one discussion design: the established question over a relevant background image. Do not alternate it with the bare question-only layout.
- Do not display vocabulary fragments or answer-like labels beneath discussion questions (for example “发行货币 issue currency”). If a difficult term needs Chinese support, integrate it selectively in the question or move it to a teaching slide/teacher notes. Avoid a full duplicate translation of the challenge.
- Keep exactly three concise bilingual objectives. Recall taught material before introducing the next lesson's content.
- A retrieval check must test content already taught at that point, including the concepts used in its distractors. Move a check later if its prerequisites have not been introduced.
- Prefer a clear image-led opening, such as recognisable commercial-bank examples, over a contrived mini-case with an unclear task. Do not add a separate scenario hook when the examples already provide one.
- Before showing an examples gallery, briefly elicit students’ own examples, e.g. “Which commercial banks do you know?” Keep the prompt before the gallery so the displayed examples do not supply their answers prematurely.
- Discussion needs room for differing justified answers: a meaningful choice, trade-off, uncertainty or changing condition. Avoid obvious prompts asking students merely to spot a funding gap or name one benefit and one cost. A section introduction may simply ask “What else do commercial banks do?” when that directly prepares the following content.
- Lay out four-mark plans as two visually aligned rows, pairing identification with explanation for the named stakeholder. Use clean left-aligned columns without decorative arrows. For Analyse questions, show development within a service: identify it → explain how it works → develop its effect. Reasoning arrows must not imply an invented mark per box.
- A complete model answer is sufficient after a practice question. Do not add a separate “Where the four marks come from” slide that repeats the model; explain creditworthy features through the model itself.

## Knowledge and images

- Show the logic through a concrete example before consolidating it in a definition.
- Definition styling is for genuine key terms such as Commercial bank, Central bank or Loan. Include a relevant photo for these concrete terms. Do not make functions such as Accepting deposits or Supervising banks look like definitions students should memorise.
- Use layouts that express the idea: labelled function/explanation tables, aligned comparisons, and causal sequences where the exam requires analysis.
- For Banking, show the commercial bank in the middle as a financial intermediary, with deposits from households and firms flowing in and loans to households and firms flowing out, including mortgage and business-investment examples. Keep this a simple intermediation explanation, not an unsupported mechanical account of money creation.
- Show recognisable major commercial banks with a picture for each, and central banks with accurately identified headquarters photos. Do not confuse Bank of China with the People's Bank of China or present examples as an unsupported size ranking.
- Avoid mock account screens such as example-bank-deposit-balance.png; use a real, relevant banking photograph rather than a manufactured screenshot.
- Use an engaging, directly relevant hero photo rather than an unappealing archival image. Preserve local image files, accurate captions, credits and source/licence links.
- Add small relevant photographs alongside individual concrete functions or knowledge points, and pictures to concrete account comparisons. Keep image rows readable and reveal them one at a time. Abstract comparisons do not need decorative photos.
- Keep function overviews concise. Present lists of products or examples as separate bilingual bullet points, e.g. Loans 贷款 and Overdrafts 透支. Remove extra benefit sentences when the function example is enough, e.g. “Arrange house or car insurance.” Develop consequences in dedicated explanations and exam models.
- Keep comparison tables to the columns needed for that teaching point. “Types of bank lending” needs the type and how it works; omit the extra Household use column.
- Written checks should use the available classroom width and a grid that matches the number of cases. For two cases, use two generous columns with readable prompts and model answers, rather than two narrow cards inside a three-column grid. Keep answer space reserved and reveal models without shifting the questions. Stack the cases on small screens.
- Design mechanism diagrams with a clear focal point, balanced stakeholder groups and clean labelled directional arrows. Avoid cramped five-column text and oversized arrow glyphs.
- Remove low-value standalone slides that repeat an overview (for example Accepting deposits); use the space for substantive explanations and exam practice.
- Use plain English and selective Chinese support in table explanations as well as headings, particularly terms such as “secured on property” (以房产作抵押). Explain the practical meaning, rather than relying on an unfamiliar technical synonym.
- Title cause-and-effect slides as explanatory relationships, e.g. “How bank lending supports investment”.
- These decks are for internal classroom teaching. Source useful pictures without adding an unsolicited copyright-permission discussion; keep accurate image metadata quietly in the source records.
