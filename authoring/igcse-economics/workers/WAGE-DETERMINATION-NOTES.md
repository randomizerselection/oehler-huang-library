# Workers Lesson 2: wage determination

Prepared 23 September 2026 as the next lesson after 3.3.1 Choice of occupation. Prepared content is not reported taught coverage.

## Teaching sequence

This 90-minute, 35-slide lesson covers the whole 2027–2029 syllabus point 3.3.2. Start with three written retrieval answers from Lesson 1, then ask why stronger bread sales could make a bakery offer a higher wage. Use the bakery throughout the labour-demand and labour-supply diagrams. Pause after the trade-union check if teaching across two periods.

1. Employers demand labour and workers supply it. Product demand creates derived demand for workers. Students predict the effects of a demand shift, then see a completed wage/employment diagram. A separate supply-shift diagram holds labour demand fixed.
2. A job interview introduces relative bargaining power. Trade unions and collective bargaining are taught explicitly, with an original Paper 1 check and full-sentence application.
3. A minimum wage is a government-set legal floor. Compare a binding floor above equilibrium with a non-binding floor below it; label labour demanded, labour supplied and the surplus. End with the original six-mark analysis question, independent writing, a separately revealed teacher model and a concise summary.
