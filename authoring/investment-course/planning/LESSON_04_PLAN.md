## Teaching precision

- Identical positive start, finish and duration give the same annualised rate despite different intermediate returns. Compare the two paths before defining the concept.
- `r = (FV / P)^(1/n) − 1` returns a decimal. Keep precision until converting to percent. End-2020 to end-2025 is five elapsed years, not six.
- The endpoint method here assumes no external cash flows and retained income. Share data include reinvested dividends; gold has no income. Benchmarks are USD nominal, before investor fees/taxes.
- Real exercises rebase published cumulative wealth to hypothetical US$1,000 investments. They are not actual students' transactions. Source calculations and image provenance are in `SOURCE-NOTES.md` and notes.
- Required, historical annualised and assumed rates answer different questions. An assumed constant rate simplifies the path; varying returns with the same cumulative factor can still produce the same endpoint.
- Hide future graph points until their step. The rate graph's labelled vertical scale starts at ¥1,800 to make differences readable.
- If calculator work needs longer, shorten graph discussion before reducing independent work or exit feedback. Report the actual stopping point if the core does not fit.

## Exit criteria

Show `(1210 / 1000)^(1/2) − 1 = 0.10 = 10%`, explain its historical equivalent-rate meaning, and name two valid forecast conditions. Verbal caution alone is insufficient evidence of the calculation skill.
