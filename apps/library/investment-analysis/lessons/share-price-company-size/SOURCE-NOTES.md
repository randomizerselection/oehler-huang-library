## Definitions and causal explanation

- [SEC Investor.gov: Market Capitalization](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/market-capitalization) and [FINRA: Market Cap Explained](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/market-cap) support the distinction between a share quote and equity market value. Outstanding shares exclude treasury stock. Market capitalisation is not sales, profits, cash or enterprise value.
- [FINRA: Stocks](https://www.finra.org/investors/investing/investment-products/stocks) supports the demand/prospects/price mechanism. The projected model now shows two concise mirrored chains: a positive surprise can raise expected profits or reduce perceived risk, increase buying and bids at the old price, and raise agreed trade prices; a negative surprise can reverse those links. Greater sales only improve profit when additional revenue exceeds additional costs. Every executed trade has both buyer and seller. The $100 → $110 trade visualization is explicitly invented, not a historical NVIDIA order book. Costco profits of $100m last year, $115m expected and $105m reported are also hypothetical: positive growth can coexist with a negative surprise.
- Three separate concept slides use local company pictures and specific examples: Costco's reported 8% increase in 2025 net sales to $269.912bn as information, the $7.15bn analyst estimate as an expectation benchmark, and Apple's revised $84bn forecast as guidance. The Costco example deliberately uses a familiar company and familiar sales data so it does not introduce stock splits before their later section. Guidance is one kind of information; investors compare it with prior expectations.

## Nintendo stock split — core knowledge

[Nintendo's 10 May 2022 announcement](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ir/pdf/2022/220510_3e.pdf), pages 1–2, documents the **10-for-1 ordinary-share split effective 1 October 2022**. This does not describe a US depositary-share ratio. The slide’s ¥60,000 / ¥6,000 prices and Emma's holding are explicitly illustrative, not historical closing prices.

Isolate the split: Emma’s 2 shares become 20; ¥60,000 becomes ¥6,000 per share; both holdings are ¥120,000. Every shareholder's count and the total outstanding count multiply by ten, preserving proportional ownership. The split itself creates no wealth or change in market cap; actual market trading can move prices for other reasons. The 2-to-20 counted circles each represent one share in Emma’s holding, not all of Nintendo.

The questions are **original teacher-written Investment practice**, not official Cambridge examination material. A dedicated calculation slide in the market-capitalisation section gives one minute of independent work before revealing Costco's $396bn model. Two later formative hinges test information surprises and splits. The final four MCQs award one mark each and assess calculation (B: $70bn), an anticipated profit increase (C: need not increase price), a negative sales-to-profit-to-price chain (A), and a split/market-cap exit (D: 880m shares, $352bn). Students record all four answers before clicking feedback. Working and oral explanations support learning, but long written-response marking is not the course default.

The Costco $900 practice, all future Costco split figures and the negative NVIDIA forecast in MCQ 3 are hypothetical. The investment-quality discussion and two explanatory questions follow Summary as optional applications. They do not consume core teaching time.

## Historical information cases

### NVIDIA, 24–25 May 2023

[NVIDIA's 24 May release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2023/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-First-Quarter-Fiscal-2024/) forecast fiscal Q2 FY2024 revenue of **$11bn ±2%**, amid demand for AI computing. [Reuters' contemporary report](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nvidia-forecasts-secondquarter-revenue-above-estimates-3090225) puts the prior Refinitiv analyst estimate at **$7.15bn**. These are forecasts for the same coming quarter; the analyst consensus is not earlier company guidance. Fiscal-year numbering does not change the May 2023 announcement date.

[AAII's closing-price report](https://www.aaii.com/investingideas/article/61396-why-nvidia-corporation8217s-nvda-stock-is-up-2437) records **$305.38 → $379.80**, a **24.37%** rise at the 25 May close. The native chart shows two daily closes indexed **100 → 124.4**, rounded to one decimal, not an invented intraday path. The revenue chart has a zero baseline and maximum $12bn. The company's ±2% qualifier remains visible. Price interpretation is explicitly an inference consistent with improved earning expectations, not proof of an isolated causal effect or a one-for-one revenue/price response.

### Apple, 2–3 January 2019

[Apple's 1 November 2018 release](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2018/11/apple-reports-fourth-quarter-results/) gave fiscal Q1 FY2019 revenue guidance of **$89–93bn**. [Its 2 January investor letter](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2019/01/letter-from-tim-cook-to-apple-investors/) cut the forecast to **about $84bn**, identifying weaker iPhone sales. This was a forecast update for the quarter ending 29 December 2018, not yet final results.

[Axios' 3 January closing report](https://www.axios.com/2019/01/03/apple-drives-stock-market-rout) records Apple's **9.96%** decline during a wider market sell-off. The price chart shows **100 → 90.0**, rounded; both company price charts use the same 0–140 scale. The revenue chart runs from zero to $100bn; its earlier-guidance bar uses the $91bn midpoint with an explicitly marked $89–93bn range, not a fabricated $91bn point forecast. Apple remained profitable. The mechanism is weaker expectations and changed willingness to trade; other information also influenced the observed daily move.

These dated cases were deliberately chosen for clear, contrasting information surprises, not as current stock recommendations. Sources checked 22 September 2026.
