# Compound growth — sources and teaching notes

Updated 22 September 2026. The active lesson now ends after the compound-growth mechanism, future-value formula and independent calculation. The unsuccessful Assumed return continuation is not part of this lesson or the pre-exam assessment scope. Its separate deck remains only as an unlinked legacy reference.

## Active learning sequence

1. Show how retained returns enlarge the balance that earns the next return.
2. Define compound growth and reinvestment.
3. Contrast simple and compound interest.
4. Introduce `FV = P(1 + r)ⁿ` and explain each term.
5. Model Emma’s savings calculation.
6. Give Lucy’s calculation as independent practice.
7. End with an individual explanation and calculation.

The 10% and 5% rates are stated conditions of fictional classroom examples. They are not current deposit rates, historical averages or forecasts. All models retain returns, add no new money and ignore fees and tax unless stated otherwise.

## Definitions

| Term | English | 中文 |
|---|---|---|
| Compound growth | Compounding is growth in which later returns build on earlier returns. | 复利增长是指后期的回报在前期回报的基础上继续产生的增长。 |
| Reinvestment | Reinvestment is using income or gains to acquire additional assets rather than withdrawing them. | 再投资是指将收入或收益用于增加资产，而不是将其取出。 |
| Future value | Future value is the final total after growth over a stated period under the stated conditions. | 终值是在给定期间和条件下，资金增长后的最终总额。 |

[Investor.gov: compound interest](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/compound-interest) supports the returns-on-prior-returns mechanism. [Investor.gov: dividend reinvestment](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/investing-your-own/direct-investing) supports acquiring additional shares with dividend income.

## Historical opening challenge

Students estimate the ending value of US$1,000 invested in 1964, matching the S&P 500 with dividends reinvested through 2025 and no additional contributions. The source is [Berkshire Hathaway’s 2025 Annual Report, pp. 19–20](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/2025ar/2025ar.pdf). Compounding its published annual returns produces US$454,694.65, displayed as approximately US$455,000.

The 1965 and 1966 observations end on 30 September; 1967 covers 15 months to 31 December. This is a hypothetical index-matching investment, not a claim that an index fund was available in 1964. Values are nominal, before investor fees and tax. Historical growth is not a promised future path.
