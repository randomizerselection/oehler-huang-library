## Real-world illustrations — 15 September 2026 follow-up

These are teaching examples, separate from the original Cambridge questions.

- **Land → rent:** [USDA NASS, Land Values and Cash Rents, August 2024](https://www.nass.usda.gov/Statistics_by_State/Regional_Office/Southern/includes/Publications/Economic_and_Demographic_Releases/Land_Value/LANDVALCR2024.pdf): US average annual cropland cash rent was $160 per acre in 2024, excluding Alaska and Hawaii. Rent is a payment for use, not the land’s sale price.
- **Labour → wages:** [UK Low Pay Commission, Minimum wage rates for 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/minimum-wage-rates-for-2025): National Living Wage of £12.21 hourly for eligible workers aged 21+ from April 2025. The slide explicitly dates the rate April 2025–March 2026 and identifies it as a minimum.
- **Uncertain profit:** [Greggs plc, 2020 preliminary results, 16 March 2021, company announcement distributed by RNS](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/greggs--grg/preliminary-results/6180938): 2019 revenue £1,167.9m and profit before tax £108.3m; 2020 revenue £811.3m and loss before tax £13.7m. 2020 covers 53 weeks, 2019 covers 52. The company reports temporary COVID-19 shop closures. This is an illustration of business uncertainty, not a controlled estimate of the effect of a revenue fall. Before-tax company profit is not cash paid to an individual entrepreneur.

The café explanation now gives teacher-written examples for each factor without changing the original stem/options. Wheat and water illustrate natural inputs; bread, flour and the café building are not used as examples of land.
