# Central banks — lesson 4

Prepared for the central-bank half of syllabus 3.1.2 after Commercial banks. Revised 14 September 2026: 31 slides; allow approximately 45 minutes and continue unfinished practice next class if needed. Prepared material is not evidence of completed teaching.

- Slides 1–3: hero, mixed recall and objectives. Slide 2 uses three wide question/model rows: commercial-bank definition, inflation and money as a store of value (spaced retrieval from Money lesson 1), and overdraft definition. Give an independent attempt before revealing each large model answer.
- Slides 4–8: introductory lending animation, headquarters examples, definition and central/commercial-bank comparison.
- Slides 9–22: one numbered functions section. 1. Issue notes and coins (10); 2. Banker to government (12); 3. Foreign currency reserves (13); 4. Lender of last resort, with emergency-lending animation and analysis together (15–16); 5. Banking regulation (18); 6. Monetary policy as a central-bank function (20). Explain each function and its economic consequences before moving on; there is no separate Importance section.
- Brief real examples follow the relevant functions: Hong Kong’s three note-issuing commercial banks (11), Northern Rock’s emergency liquidity support on 14 September 2007 (17), and UK Bank Rate rising from 0.1% in early December 2021 to 5.25% in August 2023 (21). Each has one picture, one figure/date and a short fact visible immediately. HKMA and Bank of England sources, precise dates and qualifications are recorded with the slides. Treat these as illustrations, not extra facts students must memorise for the selected questions.
- Seven teacher-written yes/no applications appear at 14, 19 and 22, replacing the simpler classification and fill-blank checks. Require an economic reason before each answer is revealed. The questions distinguish an authorised note issuer from a central bank, executing payments from deciding expenditure, reserves from interest-rate policy, liquidity from sound loans, supervision from emergency lending, slower inflation from restored purchasing power, and the price-stability aim from profitability. All prompts remain visible and each large answer/reason reveals independently and reversibly.
- Slides 23–31: past-paper practice only. Define [2] (24–25), compare [2] (26–27), Explain two functions [4] (28–29), and Analyse importance [6] (30–31). Each original question precedes its separately revealed model. Use the independent six-mark attempt as the assessed exit.

The comparison shows its column headings, feature labels and empty grid immediately, then reveals one answer cell per click. All six numbered functions now have their own prominent picture beside the accepted mark-scheme statements. Currency issue uses its qualified monopoly point; government banking shows three parallel operations; later functions develop credited causal relationships. The picture appears immediately and statements reveal separately. The generic “What it does” / “Why it matters” labels and added cash-payments/public-services benefit statements are removed. Four-mark functions use two identification/explanation pairs. Six-mark analysis follows its own scheme, not a rigid paragraph formula. The comparison question requires linked positive facts about both bank types. Quiz and flashcards cover this lesson's central-bank knowledge.

Function pictures: the existing fictional banknote-production illustration; four locally authored SVG pictures of government banking, foreign currency reserves, emergency lending and bank-risk supervision; and a Federal Reserve photograph of the January 30–31, 2024 FOMC meeting. The policy photograph is sourced from https://www.flickr.com/photos/federalreserve/53500908885/ (Federal Reserve, public domain). The SVGs illustrate the named functions rather than documenting a historical event. Image metadata is retained in the slide data; new assets are in `assets/images/central-bank-functions/`.

Photo captions distinguish the People's Bank of China from Bank of China, and the Federal Reserve Board headquarters from the whole Federal Reserve System. Central-bank institutional responsibilities vary; avoid absolute claims about supervision or currency issuance. Emergency lending is conditional. Higher interest rates may reduce inflation, not necessarily the price level.

Additional example pictures: a Bank of China (Hong Kong) HK$1,000 sample extracted from the official HKMA 2018-series leaflet, and Dominic Alves’s actual photograph of the queue outside Northern Rock in Brighton on 14 September 2007 (CC BY 2.0). The Northern Rock image illustrates the contemporary event, while the Bank of England building is an institutional illustration rather than a photograph of the policy decision.

Detailed effects on borrowing, saving, spending and inflation belong in Unit 4.3 (covered by the dedicated Interest rates deck). The former interest-rate discussion and its retail-meat price-label picture are removed.
