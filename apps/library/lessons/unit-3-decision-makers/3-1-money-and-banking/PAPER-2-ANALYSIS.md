# Banking: Paper 2 question analysis

Revised 9 September 2026. This analysis informed the teaching sequences in `slides-lesson-3.js` and `slides-lesson-4.js`, not only its final practice slides.

## Syllabus boundary

The original **Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 syllabus for 2027–2029, PDF page 16**, gives two requirements under **3.1.2 Banking**:

- role and importance of central banks
- role and importance of commercial banks

The deck therefore teaches what banks do **and what their actions enable or protect**. It includes precise service terminology and customer benefits. The local definitions reference supplies the 3.1.2 commercial-bank and central-bank wording; 3.1.1 characteristics are retained only for opening recall.

Interest rates are introduced as borrowing costs, saving rewards and a central-bank tool. Detailed monetary-policy evaluation, currency-market diagrams, household spending determinants, mergers and other neighbouring topics are not prerequisites for this deck. No eight-mark Discuss question is invented to fill a question ladder: the directly relevant selected banking questions use Identify, Define, Explain and Analyse.

## How the analysis changes the deck

1. **Knowledge is visibly connected to use and benefit.** Teaching tables read across service/function, operation and consequence. Lending products and account types are taught in the student-facing content rather than left in teacher notes.
2. **Four-mark answers have an explicit model.** The firm question is followed by paragraph-by-paragraph reveals and a mark breakdown. Later four-mark questions practise the same explanation skill in different contexts.
3. **Six-mark analysis is central.** Household services and central-bank importance receive preparation, independent writing and complete model answers before the main ending. Models develop mechanisms; they do not merely lengthen a list.
4. **The layout matches the task.** Like-for-like comparisons use tables, different rate effects use two columns, causal consequences use bilingual flows, and exam questions use their complete original wording as the title. Model paragraphs reveal separately with selected economic phrases highlighted.
5. **Independent transfer is assessed.** After modelling how one bank attracts customers, students answer the different question about consumers benefiting from more banks. The exit requires explanations rather than only isolated keywords.

## Marking cautions to teach accurately

- A four-mark **Explain two** question often uses two identifications plus two developments. The government and lending questions do not contain “two”, so the deck does not label that pattern as their only permitted structure.
- The selected six-mark **Analyse** schemes reward coherent developed content. “Three paragraphs” and “two long reasons” are useful scaffolds, not universal allocation rules. A judgement is not added as a requirement.
- In 2025ON-23 Q3(a), the first fully linked positive contrast already supplies both credited sides under the published guidance. The model gives two paired contrasts to follow the question’s wording, while making clear that the maximum is two marks.
- Loan, mortgage and overdraft are lending products. Listing them does not necessarily demonstrate three distinct service areas.
- The word **importance** requires a benefit or consequence. Repeating that a bank is important supplies no additional explanation.
- Lower **loan** rates and higher **savings** rates can attract different customers. An unspecified “higher interest rate” is ambiguous.
